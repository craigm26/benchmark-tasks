# Reset a tripped data hall EPO station

A data hall's emergency power off (EPO) station cuts power to a whole room of AI servers, and its red mushroom head
latches in when pressed. After every trip test or accidental trip, a data-center technician (O*NET 49-2011.00) or a
facilities electrician (47-2111.00) must reset it before power can be restored. On a turn-to-release head like the
Schneider Harmony XB4BS8445 used here, that means gripping the 40 mm head and turning it the way its arrows point
until the latch lets go and the head's spring lifts it, without pushing it back in (which trips it again) and without
pulling on it. The robot gets one plain instruction and must do exactly that with a parallel gripper: a level grip
on a round head in front of a flat cover, a turn about the head's own axis with no axial push, and a clean let-go
once the head has come up. The outcome is the head standing up and staying up, not a counted turn.

## What the scripted demo does and what it reads

The demo reads the fixed layout (object_jitter=0), its joint encoders and gripper opening, and one simulator value:
the head's travel, to see when the latch has let go. The right arm points its open gripper level at the wall,
stops 5 cm in front of the head, trims its wrist sag with the encoders (`trim_to_measured`), advances so the pads
straddle the skirt with the fingertips 8 mm off the cover, and closes on the 40 mm skirt (`holding()` checks the
grip; on a miss it opens, backs off and tries once more). It then rolls the wrist 0.55 rad (32 deg) clockwise, seen
from the front, one control step at a time with the hand held at the same x, so it neither pushes nor pulls. At
20 deg the latch lets go. The demo opens the hand at once, so the spring lifts the head with nothing on it, backs
straight off 8 cm, turns the wrist level again and swings the arm home with the gripper open. The left arm stays at
its ready pose. If the head has not come up after the turn (slip in the grip), it turns on to 39 deg first.

## Fidelity

Approximate. The head turns by real pad friction on its skirt. The latch is modelled: the head's 800 N/m return
spring is switched on by a scorer-side hook once the head has turned 20 deg (it then holds the head on its reset stop
with 6.2 N), and a freed head pressed fully home latches again; the demo never touches the hook. A turn-back spring of
0.15 N m/rad returns the head to 0 deg when let go. The maker publishes the 4.3 mm travel and the 44 N press force,
but no turning torque, release angle or spring force, so those are design values. The simulator scores the last step
without opening the grippers: a hand still on the head caps the score at 0.60, so a head held never scores better
than one let go.

## Run a trial

On an instance set up by the simulation walkthrough, from this folder:

```bash
INSTR="The EPO station has tripped. Reset it so it is ready for power to be restored."

# Dream-1
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=ext_dc_bess_microgrid_battery_container_service_9.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
  -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 --instruction "$INSTR" --scorer task_score \
  --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/epo-dream

# Scripted demo (closed loop, runs inside yam_scripted)
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted \
  -E task=ext_dc_bess_microgrid_battery_container_service_9.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
  -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 --instruction "$INSTR" --scorer task_score \
  --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/epo-demo
```

- **Seed and layout:** `-E object_jitter=0` is required; the station is fixed and the demo plans from its position.
- **Runtime per trial:** the demo runs about 28 s of simulated time (about 560 steps); a Dream-1 trial runs up to
  2,400 steps (2 minutes simulated). Wall-clock time on one L40S, including Isaac Sim start-up: about 80 s for the demo and about 340 s for a full Dream-1 trial.
- **Score:** `task_score` reports 1.0 once full score holds for 8 s, otherwise `score()` at the end of the run.

## Scoring

Read from the head's travel and turn angle and the grippers' distance from the head; never from forces.

1. **0.25, turned:** the head turned at least 20 deg clockwise seen from the front, which frees the latch.
2. **0.60, head up:** that, and the head at least 3.8 mm out from its latched depth (within 0.5 mm of its reset stop)
   for the final 3 s.
3. **1.00, let go:** that, and both grippers' pads at least 3 cm from the head at the end.
4. **0** if, after the head came up, it is pushed back in by more than 1.5 mm for 0.5 s (re-tripped); if the latched
   head is pulled out by more than 2 mm for 0.5 s before it was turned (forcing it); or if the station moves more
   than 2 mm. Brief nudges under 0.5 s are not counted.

Doing nothing scores 0. Possible scores: 0, 0.25, 0.60, 1.0.

**Real trial:** open both grippers, then measure the head's front from the cover face with a steel rule (latched
about 33 mm; reset about 37.5 mm, so reset means at least 3.8 mm more than the latched reading taken before the trial),
and check the turn from a front video against a paint mark on the skirt edge. A head that is up is
also free to press, which is how a technician checks it; do that only after scoring.

## Real-world build

The station is the real product, bought, not a replica: Schneider Electric Harmony XALK178E (yellow station, red
40 mm turn-to-release mushroom head, 1 NO + 1 NC; datasheet XALK178E on se.com), left unwired. `fabricate.py` writes
ASCII STL files (mm) of the two custom holding parts from the scene's dimensions: `python3 fabricate.py stl/` gives
wall_foot (print two) and drill_template.

| Schneider Electric XALK178E control station | Standard Electric Supply, item XALK178E | $116.43 |
| 12 mm plywood, 300 x 220 mm offcut, grey paint | hardware store | about $10 |
| PETG filament for two feet and the template (about 60 g) | any 1 kg spool | about $3 of a $20 spool |
| Four 3.5 x 25 mm wood screws (station), four M4 x 16 screws or clamps (feet) | hardware store | about $5 |
| **Total** | | **about $135** |

**Assembly:** print the parts; lay the drill template on the panel's front face, flush with its bottom edge, and
drill the 10 mm pilot at the station's centre; screw the station's base to the panel through its four mounting holes
inside that outline (the cable knock-outs stay closed); slide the panel into both feet and screw or clamp the feet to
the table so the cover face is 31.5 cm in front of the arm bases' midpoint.

**Reset and cleanliness:** press the head in by hand until it latches (44 N, about 4.3 mm; this is the trip). Check
that the head turns back to its rest angle. The station is not wired to any circuit, so the trial is inert and
nothing is consumed. Wipe the head with a dry cloth if the pads leave marks.
